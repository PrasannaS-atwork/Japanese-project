Culture

A primer on Japanese culture

Eight ideas that shape almost everything you will see on your trip.

和

Wa · Harmony

The oldest name for Japan itself. Group harmony comes before the individual — quiet trains, orderly queues, and an instinct to avoid friction in public.

侘寂

Wabi-sabi

Beauty in the imperfect, weathered and incomplete — a cracked tea bowl mended with gold, moss on an old stone lantern, a garden left a little wild.

神道

Shinto & Buddhism

Shinto shrines (jinja, red torii gates) honour spirits in nature; Buddhist temples (tera, pagodas and incense) came from the mainland. Most people visit both.

茶道

Sadō · The way of tea

A whole afternoon devoted to one bowl of matcha. Its motto, ichigo ichie — one time, one meeting — says every gathering happens exactly once.

祭

Matsuri · Festivals

Thousands every year: portable shrines carried shoulder-high, taiko drums, grilled squid, fireworks over a river, and giant painted floats like Aomori's nebuta.

温泉

Onsen etiquette

Wash thoroughly before you get in, leave the swimsuit behind, tie long hair up, and never let the small towel touch the water. Then stop moving and just soak.

和食

Washoku · Food

Seasonal, regional and precise: Osaka's street food, Hokkaido's seafood, Kyoto's tofu temple cuisine. Say itadakimasu before eating, and don't tip.

芸道

Arts & craft

Ukiyo-e woodblock prints, shodō calligraphy, ikebana flower arranging, origami cranes folded by the thousand as a wish for peace and recovery.
